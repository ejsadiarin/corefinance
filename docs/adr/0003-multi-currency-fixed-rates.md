# Multi-currency records with fixed rates; PHP reports

Entries were summed by `amount` alone, so a USD 100 gift counted as ₱100 in every total. Rates must be fixed at the transaction date: converting at today's rate would move past months' totals. PHP becomes the one reporting currency; each budget record keeps its original amount and currency, plus the exchange rate, PHP amount and rate date fixed when it is saved (#3).

Five decisions:

1. **PHP is the reporting currency.** Every expense and income carries its original amount and ISO 4217 currency, plus `exchange_rate` (PHP per one unit, `numeric(18,8)`), `amount_php` (centavos, `numeric(14,2)`, rounded half away from zero) and `rate_date`. PHP records use rate `1` and the record's date. Reports, filters, budget remaining and money on hand sum `amount_php`; reimbursements behave as before (ADR-0002). Rules keep only the original amount and currency; each materialized entry converts at its own due date.
2. **Rates come from frankfurter.dev (ECB).** A rate is looked up once per currency and rate date, cached, and stored on the record. A weekend or holiday uses the previous business day with a published rate. A save that cannot get a rate fails with **502** and writes nothing. Materialization writes no entry and does not advance past a due date without a rate; the next pass retries. The supported currencies are the provider's list; anything else is **400**.
3. **Editing keeps the rate unless the currency or date moves.** An amount edit with the same currency and date reuses the stored rate. Editing description, notes, category, priority or status keeps the conversion. A new currency or date fetches a new rate.
4. **One migration.** Existing PHP records get rate `1`, `amount_php = amount` and `rate_date = date`. The known 2026-07-12 gift is moved to USD at the 2026-07-10 rate **61.573**, giving `amount_php = 6157.30` and `rate_date = 2026-07-10`, matched by date and notes containing “USD 100” when present. The migration fails if it finds any other non-PHP record, because those need a historical rate it cannot invent; it skips the gift silently in dev/test databases without it.
5. **Import and export carry the conversion.** Export includes the three new fields; import trusts them and never re-fetches, so past totals cannot move. A legacy export with a non-PHP row and no conversion fields is rejected; PHP rows default to rate `1`.

**Consequences**: saving a non-PHP record depends on an external service, and a provider outage can delay rule materialization. Reports total only in PHP; per-currency breakdowns, FX gains and currency-specific decimal display are out of scope. The web presentation of the conversion is a coregateway follow-up; this repo only guarantees the API carries the fields.
